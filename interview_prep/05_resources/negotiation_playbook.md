## Comp Anatomy: What to Ask For

Always ask for the FULL breakdown:

```
Total Compensation = Base + RSU (grant ÷ vest schedule) + Sign-on + Target Bonus

Base:     Fixed monthly salary. Easiest to compare across companies.
RSUs:     Equity grant vesting over time. The BIG number at big tech.
Sign-on:  One-time payment (often in year 1 only; sometimes split Y1+Y2).
          ⚠️ Sign-on is often clawback if you leave < 1-2 years.
Bonus:    % of base, usually paid annually. "Target bonus" = if you hit your goals.
          Sometimes guaranteed for Y1; ask explicitly.
```

**Always compute the 4-year average total comp (TTC):**
```
4-yr TTC = (Base × 4) + RSU Grant + (Y1 Sign-on × 0.5 if clawback risk) + (Bonus × 4)
                        ÷ 4 years
```

Never compare companies on year-1 numbers — Amazon's Y1 will look lower than a bank's Y1 because Amazon front-loads sign-on and back-loads RSUs.

## The Amazon Vesting Trap

Amazon RSUs vest on a non-standard schedule: **5% / 15% / 40% / 40%** over 4 years.

```
Example: Rs 1.2Cr RSU grant over 4 years
  Year 1: 5%  = Rs 6L   ← almost nothing
  Year 2: 15% = Rs 18L  ← still low
  Year 3: 40% = Rs 48L  ← now it's real
  Year 4: 40% = Rs 48L

Year 1 cash = Base + Sign-on (no meaningful RSUs)
Year 2 cash = Base + small RSU tranche

→ Amazon's "Year 1 offer" feels much less than a bank offering Rs 90L base + Rs 30L bonus.
→ But by Year 3-4, Amazon's 4-yr TTC is often highest in class.
```

**When evaluating an Amazon offer:** Ask for the exact RSU grant amount, the vesting schedule, the sign-on structure (clawback timeline), and target bonus. Never compare Amazon on year-1 compensation.

## Negotiation Sequence

### 1. Get bank offers first (weeks 4-6)
- Goldman / JPMorgan loop first → get an offer
- This establishes your **floor**: "I have an offer in the Rs 75-80L range from a top-tier financial institution."
- You don't need to name the company or exact number.

### 2. Cluster Tier-1 loops in a 3-4 week window (weeks 8-11)
- Tell each company: "I'm in final rounds with several companies and expect to make a decision in the next 3-4 weeks."
- This creates urgency without fabricating competing offers.

### 3. Use offers as leverage
- Once you have 2+ offers, you can play them against each other.
- "I'm very excited about [Company A]. I currently have an offer from [Company B] at [range]. Is there anything [Company A] can do to be more competitive?"

### 4. Never anchor first if you can help it
- If asked "what are your compensation expectations?": "I'm more interested in understanding the full compensation structure for this role. Can you share the range?"
- If they push: "I've done my research on levels.fyi and I know senior engineers at your company in India earn in the Rs [range]. I'd expect to be in that range."

## Scripts (Copy-Paste Ready)

### Script 1: Asking for the Full Breakdown

*"Thank you so much for the offer — I'm really excited about this role. Before I can respond, I want to make sure I'm comparing apples to apples across the opportunities I'm considering. Could you share the full breakdown — base salary, RSU grant and vesting schedule, sign-on structure (including any clawback terms), and target bonus? I want to understand the 4-year total comp picture."*

---

### Script 2: Countering an Offer

*"Thank you for putting together this offer. I'm genuinely excited about the role and the team. I've been reviewing it against my research and the competing offer I'm currently evaluating, and I was hoping you could move to [Rs X] on [base / RSU grant]. Is that something you can do? I'd love to be able to say yes quickly."*

**Notes:**
- State a specific number. "A bit more" doesn't work.
- "Competing offer" doesn't need to be from a specific named company.
- "I'd love to say yes quickly" signals genuine interest and creates urgency.

---

### Script 3: Stalling for a Competing Offer

*"I really appreciate the offer and I'm genuinely excited about [Company]. I'm currently in final rounds with another company and I'm expecting their decision by [date]. Would it be possible to give me until [date + 3 business days] to give you my answer? I want to make a fully informed decision and I want to be respectful of your timeline."*

**Notes:**
- Always give a specific date — "a few more days" invites pressure.
- Add 3 business days to their expected decision date for safety.
- Never lie about having an offer you don't have.

---

### Script 4: Asking for Sign-On

*"The base and RSU structure look very competitive. I do have a Rs [X]L sign-on at [current/competing company] that I'd be forgoing. Is there flexibility on a one-time sign-on to bridge that gap? Something in the Rs [X] range would make the decision much easier."*

**Notes:**
- Sign-on is often the easiest thing for companies to move on.
- Banks especially use sign-on as a negotiating lever.
- Always ask — the worst they can say is no.

---

### Script 5: Never Accept on the Call

*"This sounds really exciting and I'm very interested. Can you put it in writing so I can review the full details? I want to make sure I'm fully informed before I say yes — I hope you understand I need to see the complete offer letter to do that."*

**Golden rule:** NEVER accept on the call. Verbal acceptance is not legally binding in most contexts, but it creates social pressure that limits your negotiation leverage. Always ask for it in writing.

## Key Rules

```
✅ Always ask for the full breakdown (base / RSU / sign-on / bonus / clawback)
✅ Always compute 4-year average TTC — never compare on year-1
✅ Use "I have a competing offer in the range of..." — you don't need to name the company
✅ Always stall by asking for it in writing
✅ Ask for sign-on — it's often easier to move than base or RSU
✅ Never badmouth your current employer or a competing company
✅ Leave every door open — decline gracefully if you have to

❌ Never anchor first with your current salary (you're at 54L — don't let that anchor them)
❌ Never accept on the call
❌ Never accept Amazon SDE II — insist on L6/SDE III
❌ Never accept a bank offer below Rs 75-80L (use it only for leverage below that)
❌ Never fabricate an offer that doesn't exist
```
